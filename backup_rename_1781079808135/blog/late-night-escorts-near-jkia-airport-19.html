2. Late-Night Transport – What Works, What Doesn’t

  • ✅ Uber / Bolt (Recommended): Available 24/7. Drivers accept cash or M‑Pesa. For late‑night arrivals, order from the designated pick-up zone outside Terminal 1 or Terminal 2. Drivers will use the Expressway, which is well‑lit and patrolled.
  • ✅ Private Airport Transfer: Some services (e.g., ETA, 24/7) offer fixed‑rate transfers on the Expressway. Reliable for passengers who prefer a pre‑booked car.
  • ❌ Matatu (Public Bus): Not available after 9 pm. The last matatu from JKIA leaves around 8 pm–8:35 pm. Do not rely on it for midnight arrivals.
  • ❌ Boda Boda: Not recommended after dark on Mombasa Road, especially with luggage. Drivers may overcharge or take unsafe routes.
  • ❌ Street Taxi (unbooked): Avoid unofficial taxis inside the airport. They often overcharge (KSh 1,500–3,000) and safety is not guaranteed. Stick to Uber/Bolt or pre‑booked services.

5. Late‑Night Safety Checklist

  • Use Uber or Bolt – not street taxis. The cost difference is small, and ride‑hailing apps provide tracking and driver details.
  • Share your live location with a trusted friend, especially if you are going to an Airbnb or private apartment.
  • Confirm the companion’s availability before ordering an Uber. WhatsApp or call to verify that she is awake and ready. Late‑night no‑shows are a risk – double‑check.
  • Carry small notes (KSh 200, 500, 1000). Drivers and some companions may not have change for large denominations after midnight.
  • If a companion asks for a deposit, keep it reasonable. For late‑night outcalls, a deposit of KSh 500–1,000 is standard. Never send the full amount before meeting.
  • Stick to well‑lit areas. If an incall address is vague or in a poorly lit street, ask the companion to meet you at a nearby petrol station (Total or Kobil) first.
