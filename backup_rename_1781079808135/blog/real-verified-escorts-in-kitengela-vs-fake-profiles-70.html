1. The Scale of the Problem – Fake Escort Ads Are Everywhere

Fake escort profiles are not rare. According to a 2022 investigation, nearly 70% of escort ads on some platforms were found to be fake or outdated[reference:0]. In Kenya, scam networks are sophisticated – they operate through WhatsApp, Facebook, Telegram, and classified sites. Some use stolen photos, others use automated chatbots, and the most dangerous ones are actual robbery crews posing as escorts.

In one recent Nairobi case, two men were arrested after posing as female massage therapists online. They lured a client with the promise of an in‑home massage – then robbed him of KSh 280,000 at knifepoint[reference:1]. This is not a theoretical risk. It happens.

5. Your Safety Checklist – Before You Book

  • ✓ Reverse image search profile photos. Use Google Images or TinEye. If the same photo appears under different names or on multiple escort sites in different cities, it is stolen[reference:11].
  • ✓ Ask for a live video call or a specific selfie. For example: “Send me a photo holding up three fingers.” A real escort can do this in seconds. A scammer using stolen photos cannot[reference:12].
  • ✓ Never send money before meeting. A deposit may be requested for overnight bookings, but never the full amount. If they ask for “registration fee”, “health certificate fee”, or any other upfront charge, walk away. Those are classic scams[reference:13].
  • ✓ For incall, verify the address first. Does it look like a real apartment building? Can you see it on Google Maps Street View? If the address is vague or the area looks abandoned, do not go.
  • ✓ Meet in a public place first. The DCI has warned against having first dates at private residences. A well‑lit petrol station, a hotel lobby, or a busy food court gives you a chance to assess before committing[reference:14].
  • ✓ Share your live location with a trusted friend. Tell someone where you are going and the companion‘s contact details. This simple step has saved lives.
  • ✓ Trust your gut. If something feels off – the conversation is weird, the address is strange, the price is too good – cancel. There will always be other profiles.
Photo verification tip: Real escort photos usually have an authentic quality. Images that look like they came straight from a modelling shoot are a red flag[reference:15]. If every photo is perfectly lit, styled, and professional, someone else probably owns that face.

6. What to Do If You‘ve Been Scammed

  • For M‑Pesa fraud: Call Safaricom customer care (100) immediately. Report the transaction. They may be able to freeze the recipient‘s line. However, voluntary payments are rarely refunded – which is why prevention is critical.
  • For robbery or assault: Go to the nearest police station. Kitengela Police Station is located near the main stage along Namanga Road. File a report with the phone number, address, and any other details.
  • Report the profile: If you found the ad on a platform, report it as a scam. Share the phone number on community forums (anonymously) to warn others.
  • Warn others: Local WhatsApp and Telegram groups for nightlife in Kitengela exist. Post a brief warning (without sharing your identity) to alert other potential victims.
