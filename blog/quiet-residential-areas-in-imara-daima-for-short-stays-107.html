Imara Daima is a peaceful, well-planned residential neighbourhood in Nairobi’s Embakasi South constituency, approximately 8.2 km (5.1 mi) south of the CBD[reference:0]. It is bordered by Fedha Estate, Kware, Dolholm, Pipeline Estate, and Mukuru Kwa Njenga slums[reference:1]. While most visitors know Imara Daima for the convenience of Imaara Shopping Mall and its proximity to JKIA, the area is also home to some of the quietest, most secure gated communities in the region — ideal for travellers seeking a restful short stay or a discreet overnight retreat.

1. Why Imara Daima for a Short Stay?

Imara Daima is characterised by gated estates, well-paved roads, and modern apartments that blend privacy with city living[reference:2]. Its strategic location makes it attractive for several reasons:

  • Security: Almost all apartments are covered by private guards. Some property managers have installed CCTV cameras, and there are two police posts (Imara Daima Police Post and Villa Franka Police Post)[reference:3].
  • Accessibility: 15 minutes to Nairobi CBD via Mombasa Road or Expressway, direct access to SGR Syokimau Station and JKIA, and serviced by matatus (route 33 and 110), boda bodas, and taxi apps[reference:4].
  • Self-contained living: Modern shopping malls (Southfield Mall, Imaara Mall), health centres, restaurants, and gyms are all within easy reach without the noise of the city centre[reference:5].
  • Peaceful atmosphere: Imara Daima offers a quiet, suburban feel with well-maintained streets and green spaces — far from the chaotic nightlife of Kitengela or the heavy truck traffic of Mlolongo[reference:6].
Security note: While Imara Daima is generally safe, its bordering areas include the Mukuru Kwa Njenga informal settlement. Always choose accommodation inside well‑established gated communities, and avoid walking alone on isolated side streets after dark.

4. Security & Privacy Considerations

Imara Daima is known for its good security, with most apartments covered by private guards and many property managers installing CCTV cameras[reference:11]. However, the visitor policies of these gated communities can vary significantly.

  • Gate entry: Most gated communities require visitors to sign in at the gatehouse. Whenever possible, residents should notify security in advance of pending visitors to avoid issues[reference:12].
  • ID registration: Some estates require visitors to present a government ID for logging. This is standard for gated communities in Kenya and not usually intrusive.
  • Recommended approach for discreet guests: Opt for an Airbnb with self check‑in to skip front‑desk interactions entirely. Alternatively, choose a larger gated community where visitor registration is routine and not overly scrutinised (e.g., Kings Millennium).

5. Getting Around – Transport & Amenities

Despite its quiet residential feel, Imara Daima is well connected.

  • By Matatu: Indimanje Sacco matatus operate from Bus Station (BS) next to Afya Centre in Nairobi CBD to St Bakhita, with drop‑offs at Villa Franka, Imara Daima Estate, and Mastermind. The fare is KES 100, with a travel time of 45 min to 1 hour during peak periods and 20‑30 minutes off‑peak[reference:13][reference:14].
  • By Train: Imara Daima Railway Station offers morning and evening services to Nairobi CBD. The ticket price is usually KES 40. Once you alight, a boda boda to your house costs about KES 50[reference:15].
  • By Uber/Bolt: Ride‑hailing apps are widely available and are the safest option for late‑night returns.
  • Nearby amenities: Imaara Shopping Mall (7‑min walk) has dining and retail. Southfield Mall and Naivas Embakasi are also nearby[reference:16][reference:17].
Commute note: If you are driving, the Nairobi Expressway makes reaching the CBD or JKIA incredibly fast — around 15 minutes from Imara Daima.

7. Practical Tips for Short Stays in Imara Daima

  • For maximum privacy: Choose an Airbnb with self check‑in (lockbox/keypad) to avoid front‑desk interactions.
  • If using a hotel-style apartment: Inform them that you are expecting a visitor. Most gated communities allow visitors but require them to sign in at the gate[reference:18].
  • Carry a government ID: Some estates require visitor registration — standard procedure in Kenya.
  • For late arrivals: Call ahead to confirm check‑in procedures. Kings Millennium and other large estates have 24‑hour security and will still allow entry, but smaller blocks may have cut‑offs.
  • Stock up at Imaara Mall: The mall is a short walk from most apartments and has Naivas supermarket, restaurants, and a food court.
